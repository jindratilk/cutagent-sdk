# Sound design

Make the viewer feel the edit without hearing a pile of effects. Every sound should clarify an action, establish a place, shape a transition, or focus attention.

## Map sonic roles

Watch the cut once without adding effects. Mark only moments that need one of these roles:

- **world:** ambience, room tone, traffic, wind, crowd, machine bed;
- **action:** foley that makes contact, movement, handling, or texture believable;
- **interface:** restrained clicks, typing, camera, notification, or confirmation sounds;
- **motion:** whoosh or movement texture matched to direction and speed;
- **impact:** a hit, thump, snap, or bass accent for a real visual or narrative arrival;
- **transition:** rise, reverse, tape, suction, or tonal bridge that prepares a change;
- **motif:** a recurring sound family that gives the piece identity.

Leave quiet moments quiet. Do not add an effect to every cut, title, or zoom.

## Choose and layer sounds

Read `references/supplied-assets.md` when the active skill exposes the supplied cue set. Select by role and texture, not by filename alone. Audition the actual candidate and verify its rights for the intended use.

Start with one sound that carries the moment. Add layers only when they contribute different information: transient, body, tail, or environment. Avoid stacking several similar impacts or whooshes. Vary repeated cues subtly so the edit does not sound stamped.

Match motion with timing and envelope. A fast small movement needs a shorter, lighter cue than a large camera move. Let risers and reverse sounds begin early enough to create anticipation; place impacts on the perceived contact frame, then shape the tail around the next line or cut.

## Integrate the mix

Keep dialogue and meaningful production sound in front. Use spectrum, stereo position, and duration as well as gain to make space. Ambience should connect edits rather than restart at every shot. Preserve perspective: distant actions should not sound close and oversized.

For local execution, read `cutagent-projects`, `cutagent-audio`, `cutagent-editing`, `cutagent-verification`; check the installed SDK declarations, public CutAgent CLI command cards, and live capabilities for exact operations.

## Review

Audition the full pass, then dialogue-only conflicts, the busiest sequence, repeated cue families, transitions, and the ending. Check sync, clicks, hard tails, masking, excessive low end, stereo distraction, and whether the sound still works at low volume. Structural readback proves placement; audition proves taste and integration.

Report the cue map, assets and rights, layered moments, ambience strategy, mix treatment, and listening evidence.
