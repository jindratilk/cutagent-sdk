# Supplied sound assets

The supplied creative asset set includes short cue families suitable for original sound-design work: camera shutters and camera handling, mouse clicks, typing, paper and money handling, swooshes, a riser, reverse piano, tape/cassette movement, notification/confirmation tones, charging/connection tones, and other interface sounds.

Treat these as source recordings, not as a preset edit. Inspect the files actually exposed with the active skill, audition them, verify rights, and choose only the cue whose texture fits the moment. The set may vary; do not invent a filename or assume every family is present.

Useful combinations include:

- shutter transient plus a quieter mechanical body for a camera action;
- short click or notification tone for a real interface confirmation;
- paper texture under a document/card movement;
- one directional whoosh shaped to a graphic move;
- reverse piano or riser leading into a reveal;
- tape insertion/ejection texture for an archival or analog transition;
- typing loop trimmed to the actual visible keystroke rhythm.

Keep layers editable on separate, role-named audio tracks until the balance is approved. Trim silence, add short fades, preserve natural tails, and vary repeated cues through source choice or subtle timing—not random heavy processing. Read `cutagent-audio` for placement and mix mechanics.

## Find a local cue

Use a sound effect supplied by the user, already in the project, or available in a local licensed library. Check its origin and reuse rights. Audition the actual file against the picture and dialogue before importing it through the installed CutAgent SDK or CutAgent CLI. If no suitable cue exists, report the missing input instead of assuming a remote catalog.
