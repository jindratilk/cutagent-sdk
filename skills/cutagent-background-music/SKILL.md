# Background music

Give every cue a narrative job. Silence is part of the arrangement, and dialogue remains the anchor whenever it is present.

## Build the cue map

Listen to the cut as a sequence of ideas and energy changes. Mark the hook, first explanation, chapter turns, demonstrations, montage, emotional pivot, conclusion, and call to action. For each region decide whether music should lead, support, recede, or disappear.

Choose a mode from the material rather than habit:

- **Minimal:** interviews, intimate stories, sensitive subjects, and naturalistic scenes. Reserve music for framing, transitions, or a genuine emotional turn.
- **Balanced:** most educational, documentary, product, and creator videos. Use a small family of cues with clear rises and releases.
- **Music-driven:** trailers, ads, montages, and rhythmic short-form. Let phrases and accents influence the picture without cutting on every beat.

Do not add a new cue for every section. Change music when the story's emotion, environment, time, or energy actually changes.

## Choose the track

Define the cue's emotional meaning, energy, vocal tolerance, useful duration, density around speech, and preferred ending before searching. Read `references/asset-selection.md` when selecting supplied or locally licensed music.

Audition at most three plausible candidates through the available preview path. Judge the whole usable region: opening, development, phrase structure, dynamics, edit points, and ending. Reject a track that manufactures drama, creates accidental comedy, masks speech, or feels interchangeable with generic stock music.

## Shape the edit

Enter on a motivated visual or narrative event. Leave on a cadence, decay, designed stop, or deliberate transition to silence. When the source length does not fit, prefer an alternate mix, a stronger source region, a phrase-aligned removal, or a natural loop. Preserve a musical ending instead of fading every cue by default.

Use levels and ducking as phrasing, not as a permanent blanket reduction. Lower before important speech begins, remain down through the complete thought, and recover after it resolves. Let music lead only where the story can spare the attention. Preserve production sound when it carries place, action, or authenticity.

For local execution, read `cutagent-audio`, `cutagent-editing`, `cutagent-verification`; check the installed SDK declarations, public CutAgent CLI command cards, and live capabilities for exact operations.

## Review

Check the entrance, the densest dialogue, every structural music edit, a transition, and the ending. Confirm the cue still serves the story at low playback volume and on ordinary speakers. Structural readback can prove placement and settings; only audition can prove balance, masking, pumping, or emotional fit.

Report the selected source and rights, cue map, structural edits, dialogue treatment, proof reached, and any listening decision that remains.
