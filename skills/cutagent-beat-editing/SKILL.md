# Beat editing

Edit to musical structure, not every beat. Music should strengthen story, action, readability, and emotion rather than turn the sequence into a metronome.

## Map the music and footage

Identify the driving cue, editable picture, protected dialogue or sync, and whether order or duration may change. Review the whole cue and distinguish section changes, phrase boundaries, downbeats, builds, drops, breaks, pauses, and final accents. Use actual analysis or audition evidence; a waveform, duration, or filename does not prove musical structure.

Review the footage for natural action starts, peaks, reveals, reactions, camera moves, usable handles, and minimum readable duration. Treat the existing edit as intentional evidence and preserve its story unless restructuring is requested.

## Assign visual weight

Reserve the strongest visual change for the strongest musical event. A new section can introduce a scene or idea; a phrase start can carry a normal cut; a transient can land an action, graphic, or detail; a pause can create stillness; the final accent should resolve the picture.

Let motion begin before the beat when its contact, reveal, or completion should land on it. Use smaller beats for internal movement, light changes, or graphics instead of cutting every time.

Shape density as a curve:

- hold longer through calm passages;
- vary shot length through ordinary sections;
- accelerate selectively through a build;
- make the drop decisive;
- release density in a breakdown or ending.

Protect dialogue, legible titles, emotional holds, and complete actions even when that means using the nearest suitable musical point.

## Build the pass

Plan large structure before trims. Map each important musical point to one intended visual event and check source bounds, continuity, and readability. Then work from macro to micro: sections and scene changes, shot boundaries and action peaks, followed by selective transitions, speed changes, or graphic accents.

Favor clean cuts and varied patterns. Use match cuts, motion transitions, flashes, or speed ramps only when supported by the image and established style. An effect should reinforce rhythm, not announce itself on every beat.

## Execute from current technical guidance

For local execution, read `cutagent-editing`, `cutagent-verification`, `cutagent-audio`; check the installed SDK declarations, public CutAgent CLI command cards, and live capabilities for exact operations.

Apply the plan to the authorized timeline or an appropriate reversible copy. After any duration-changing pass, re-check every later musical relationship instead of repairing only the final clip. Preserve linked audio and protected tracks.

## Review

Audition the complete sequence when judging musical feel. Confirm that sections, builds, drops, pauses, and the ending carry the intended weight; shot lengths vary; action peaks land naturally; and speech remains clear. Review the picture without sound as well: it should retain continuity, legibility, and visual development.

Structural readback can prove edit positions, but not groove. Report the musical evidence used, pacing approach, major alignments, preserved content, visual review, audition status, and any unresolved handles, sync, or capability limitation.
