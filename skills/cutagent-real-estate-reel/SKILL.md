# Real-estate reel

Sell the experience of moving through the property while keeping every claim and spatial relationship honest. Let architecture, light, view, and material detail lead; effects should support the tour.

## Build the property story

Inventory exterior, arrival, entry, main living space, kitchen, bedrooms, bathrooms, amenities, details, and outlook. Note time of day, camera direction, floor or zone, movement quality, and strongest reveal in each shot.

Choose a narrative appropriate to the footage:

- arrival to interior to view for a spatial tour;
- hero feature first, then supporting spaces for a short reel;
- lifestyle rhythm built from light, texture, amenities, and surroundings;
- architectural progression from scale to craftsmanship to final outlook.

Do not imply a room connection, view, feature, price, size, location, or amenity that the supplied evidence does not support.

## Select and order shots

Prefer shots with clean movement, readable geometry, controlled exposure, and a clear beginning and end. Establish enough wide context before a run of details. Match doorways, movement direction, horizon, color temperature, and brightness so the viewer can follow the space.

Vary scale: exterior or room reveal, medium architectural feature, close material detail, then release to another wide. Reserve the strongest drone, pool, terrace, mountain, ocean, or skyline view for an early promise or final payoff rather than spending every hero image at once.

Use speed changes only on stable motion with sufficient source quality. Hide rough turns with a motivated cut, not an arbitrary transition. Let premium spaces breathe; use faster detail bursts only where music and shot complexity support them.

Build sequences in spatial phrases rather than shuffled highlights. A useful phrase is: establish the zone, cross its threshold, reveal the main volume, show one material or functional detail, then exit toward the next zone. Match the outgoing movement or architectural line to the incoming shot. When geography is uncertain, reset with an exterior, wide room, landing, or view instead of implying a false connection.

For a short vertical reel, a strong starting shape is hero promise, arrival, two or three interior phrases, amenity or detail burst, and final outlook. Allocate more time to complex wides than to simple details. Crop each shot deliberately around doors, counters, horizon, and vertical lines; a centered automatic crop can destroy the architecture.

## Add type, music, and finish

Use sparse, editable typography for verified facts such as property name, location, room, or feature. Keep labels off important architecture and within vertical safe areas. Create original Text+ and Fusion animation that fits the property's visual language; do not relabel a purchased real-estate template.

Build a callout from one Text+ branch and one thin line or translucent panel branch. Anchor it to stable negative space, not to a moving architectural edge. Use a short masked line reveal followed by the label, hold through the relevant shot, and remove both before the next room. Reuse the system with new measured placement rather than pasting the same coordinates across shots.

Choose music whose energy matches the property and audience. Align major reveals to phrases or section changes, not every beat. Preserve useful natural sound when it makes an arrival, water feature, fireplace, or door interaction more tangible.

For local execution, read `cutagent-editing`, `cutagent-fusion`, `cutagent-audio`, `cutagent-verification`; check the installed SDK declarations, public CutAgent CLI command cards, and live capabilities for exact operations.

## Review

Watch the complete reel for spatial clarity, repeated angles, movement direction, exposure jumps, vertical crops, title collisions, and an ending that resolves the tour. Audition the music, transitions, and natural sound. Confirm every visible fact against supplied property information.

Report the story arc, hero moments, shot order, original graphics, verified property claims, picture and audition review, and any missing coverage or fact that limited the result.
