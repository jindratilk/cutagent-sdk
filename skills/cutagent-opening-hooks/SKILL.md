# Opening hooks

Earn attention with a clear idea, not a borrowed preset. Build the opening from the source's strongest promise, tension, proof, surprise, or unanswered question, then author the typography and motion specifically for that idea.

## Find the hook

Identify what the viewer should want to know after the first beat. Useful structures include:

- result first, followed by how it happened;
- a credible contradiction or unexpected claim;
- problem, consequence, and promised answer;
- striking proof before explanation;
- a visual transformation or incomplete action;
- a direct question with real stakes.

Keep enough context to remain truthful. Do not manufacture urgency, remove a necessary qualifier, or promise a payoff the edit does not deliver.

## Compose the opening

Break the hook into a few readable visual beats. Give each beat one job: establish, intensify, reveal, or resolve into the body. Use short lines, deliberate breaks, strong word hierarchy, and a settled frame that works before animation.

Let picture and type alternate in dominance. A bold phrase can interrupt the opening, a proof image can replace it, and a quieter label can bridge into the story. Avoid stacking several equally loud titles, effects, and sounds at once.

Animate original Text+, shapes, masks, and images. Use stagger or per-word emphasis only when it follows speech or reading order. Blur, slide, scale, wipe, or texture should communicate the transition between ideas. Do not import a purchased hook sequence and change its wording.

Use risers, impacts, tape textures, or other supplied sounds only when licensed and editorially justified. Align sound to a reveal or transition, then audition it against dialogue and music.

### Build one of these original structures

**Stack and resolve:** Split a short phrase across two to four Text+ nodes. Keep a shared alignment and width, but vary scale or weight so one phrase owns the frame. Reveal from back to front with small vertical offsets, then clear the stack as one grouped branch into the first picture. The supplied Viral Hooks project uses stacked Fusion titles as a useful grammar; rebuild the arrangement and timing for the actual message rather than copying its titles.

**Blur-up sequence:** Give each line its own Text+ and Transform, then place a Blur before the line's merge. Start slightly below with blur and low opacity, ease to a sharp settled line, hold, and exit upward or dissolve into the next line. Overlap consecutive lines enough to preserve reading continuity, but never leave two equally important phrases fighting for attention. The pack's named “Blur Up slide” is evidence for this motion family, not a graph to duplicate.

**Proof interruption:** Open on picture, interrupt with a full-frame claim, then reveal the proof image or clip through a mask or decisive cut. Return to the speaker or body only after the proof is readable. Use an accent shape or one highlighted word to connect the claim to the proof.

For a compact hook, treat duration proportionally: establish the premise in the first 20–25%, intensify or contradict by 45–55%, show proof or the unanswered question by 70–80%, then bridge into the body. Copy length and speech timing control the real frames. A riser can span the build; an impact belongs on the reveal; tape or cassette texture is best reserved for a genuine temporal or media-style shift.

Construct text families from editable branches—Text+ plus optional Transform, Blur, and mask—then merge related lines into a group before global motion. Keep long supporting copy separate from the short hero phrase so it can remain smaller, enter later, and be removed for tighter variants.

## Build through current technical guidance

For local execution, read `cutagent-fusion`, `cutagent-editing`, `cutagent-audio`, `cutagent-verification`; check the installed SDK declarations, public CutAgent CLI command cards, and live capabilities for exact operations.

Keep the authored Fusion graph or `.setting` editable and preserve the source edit. Match timing to actual speech, gesture, music, and picture rather than to a preset's original duration.

## Review

Watch the opening into the first body section. The first beat should orient quickly, the central tension should be understandable without prior context, every word should remain legible, and the transition into the video should feel intentional rather than like a detached bumper.

Report the hook proposition, beat structure, original typography and motion, assets used, visual and audition review, and any claim or timing that still needs user approval.
