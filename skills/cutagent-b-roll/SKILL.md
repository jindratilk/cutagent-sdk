# B-roll

Every insert must clarify meaning, reveal context, carry emotion, bridge continuity, or improve pacing. A changing picture is not a reason by itself.

## Read the message before choosing pictures

Review the relevant source in chronological context. Use the transcript when speech carries the edit; use motion and sound when the material is visual. Divide it into claims, examples, processes, emotional turns, locations, products, topic changes, and picture edits that need coverage.

For each useful opportunity, state:

- what the viewer should understand or feel;
- why the speaker or primary action should temporarily leave the screen;
- the best visual function: literal, process, contextual, emotional, metaphorical, product, archival, screen, or transitional;
- the approximate entry, exit, and return-to-A-roll point.

Judge the complete thought rather than matching isolated words. Prefer a specific process or consequence to a generic visual cliché. Keep important testimony, reactions, punchlines, and direct address visible.

## Find the strongest available shot

Search existing project media and user-provided files before external sources. Review candidates in motion and choose a deliberate subrange with a readable action or composition. Compare semantic fit, authenticity, light, framing, motion direction, visual quality, aspect-ratio fit, and continuity with neighboring shots.

Use supplied or locally licensed footage only when its rights and intended use are clear. If a needed shot is missing, identify the gap and ask for suitable footage rather than inserting a weak substitute.

Avoid consecutive near-duplicates and track repeated source use. If no available shot genuinely helps, leave the A-roll visible.

## Shape coverage as a sequence

Choose density from the content. Personal or emotional material benefits from restraint; demonstrations and fast explainers can support denser, shorter inserts. Every shot still needs enough time to read.

Enter when the idea becomes understandable, not mechanically at sentence boundaries. A shot may anticipate a mention, span a picture edit, or end early to reveal a reaction. Sequence changes in shot size, direction, brightness, and movement so the B-roll reads as a small visual argument rather than a pile of clips.

Favor clean cuts. Add transitions, reframing, speed changes, or natural sound only when they strengthen continuity or meaning. Do not use effects to compensate for a weak shot.

## Place without damaging the edit

For local execution, read `cutagent-editing`, `cutagent-verification`; check the installed SDK declarations, public CutAgent CLI command cards, and live capabilities for exact operations.

Work on the authorized timeline or a reversible copy appropriate to the scope. Inspect the full compositing stack over each range so B-roll remains above primary picture and below protected captions, titles, or graphics. Preserve linked dialogue unless natural sound is an intentional part of the plan.

## Review

Watch the complete covered passage. Remove inserts that repeat the words without adding value, obscure a needed face, arrive before their meaning, overstay, clash with adjacent movement, or introduce unintended sound. Confirm the intended timeline and neighboring overlays structurally, then inspect representative output. Audition whenever claiming that natural sound or dialogue transitions work.

Report what was selected and placed, the purpose of each insert, its source and rights, any media limitation, and what visual or listening review remains.
