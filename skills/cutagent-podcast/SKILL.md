# Podcast

Cut for understanding and human connection, not constant activity. Speaker identity, synchronization, and programme audio must remain coherent even when the visual rhythm becomes more expressive.

## Map the conversation

Identify every camera, isolated microphone, speaker, wide angle, and useful reaction angle. Establish a trustworthy sync relationship before judging the edit. Read `references/visual-sync-method.md` when scratch audio is absent or unreliable.

Outline the episode by hook, premise, chapters, stories, disagreements, demonstrations, and conclusion. Use a transcript to understand meaning and speaker turns, but not as a substitute for synchronization or listening.

## Design the camera language

Let the active speaker carry the default shot. Use the wide for real overlaps, shared laughter, physical interaction, or a deliberate reset. Hold on a listener only when the reaction adds meaning; do not manufacture reactions by stealing them from another moment.

Avoid cutting on every change of voice. Allow a thought to settle, protect punchlines and emotional pauses, and vary holds according to energy. A short interjection may not deserve a cut; a major change in argument or emotion often does.

Read `references/calibration-and-planning.md` before tuning an automatic speaker plan. Treat the plan as a first edit: review the opening, dense exchanges, overlaps, long silences, and ending before applying it.

## Build the programme sound

Choose one clear microphone-to-speaker mapping and keep it independent from camera switching. Preserve genuine overlap, laughter, breaths, and room perspective. Remove doubled scratch audio unless it has an intentional role. Use short, natural handoffs and keep room tone continuous.

Read `references/program-audio-and-verification.md` before placing isolated microphones or replacing sources.

For local execution, read `cutagent-multicam`, `cutagent-audio`, `cutagent-editing`, `cutagent-verification`; check the installed SDK declarations, public CutAgent CLI command cards, and live capabilities for exact operations.

## Review

Review early, middle, late, overlap, silence, and ending sections. Check that every cut has valid source media, the chosen angle matches the conversational intention, the audio source belongs to the visible or intended speaker, and sync remains stable over time. Structural readback proves mapping; playback proves rhythm and programme-audio continuity.

Report source assignments, sync authority, editorial rules, programme-audio design, representative proof, and any reaction or overlap that still needs human judgment.
