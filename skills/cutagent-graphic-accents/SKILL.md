# Graphic accents

Use accents as visual verbs: point, circle, underline, connect, frame, or punctuate. Construct them from editable Fusion shapes, masks, text, and animation rather than copying decorative assets from a purchased pack.

## Give every accent a target

Name what the viewer should notice and what the accent communicates. An arrow identifies direction, a circle isolates, an underline stresses language, a line connects ideas, and a star or burst punctuates a moment. Remove an accent that does not improve attention or meaning.

Fit the mark to the delivery format and image. Keep it clear of faces, captions, controls, and platform crop zones. Match the scene's type and palette, but maintain enough contrast to read over changing footage. Prefer one strong accent family over several unrelated shapes.

## Construct a coherent mark

Build simple geometry with deliberate proportions: consistent stroke width, compatible corner character, clean arrowheads, and balanced negative space. Keep fill, outline, glow, and shadow as separate editable decisions. When the accent labels an object, group the mark and label so they move together while preserving readable spacing.

Choose a motion appropriate to the verb:

- Draw a line, circle, or underline in the direction the eye should travel.
- Pop a star or burst with one scale overshoot, then hold still.
- Slide or rotate an arrow just enough to establish direction.
- Use a brief flicker or stepped reveal only for a deliberately rough or urgent style.
- Let repeated accents share timing and proportions, with a small stagger only when it communicates order.

Introduce the accent just before or with the referenced word or visual action. Hold it long enough for the viewer to connect mark and target. Remove it before the next competing idea unless it remains useful context. Secondary glow, blur, or shake should decay quickly; the target, not the accent, stays primary.

## Review

Inspect the frame before the accent, its fully visible state, and its removal. Confirm that the mark points to the intended target, remains legible over the footage, does not obscure protected content, and keeps consistent stroke and edge quality. Review drawing direction, pop timing, and any stagger in motion; a still cannot prove them.

Use structural readback to confirm the editable graph and animation data, then use representative frames or a temporal preview for placement and motion quality.
